1. Interconnection Networks and Protocols Used for Cache-Coherence

Author: Swetang Gajjar | Institution: California State University, Fullerton | Domain: Computer Architecture

Abstract: In multi-core processors sharing memory, write operations can lead to synchronization conflicts and dirty caches across concurrent executing threads. This paper analyzes the cache coherence problem and evaluates Snooping-based, Directory-based, and Distributed Shared Memory protocols. By examining how memory states are updated and duplicated across interconnection topologies, the study models latency optimization, data invalidation overhead, and directory-scale constraints under high processor concurrency.

Cache Coherence Directory Protocols Snooping Interconnection Networks Multi-Core Systems

2. Transition Delay Fault Testing & Low-Power 8-Bit Ripple Carry Adder

Author: Swetang Gajjar | Institution: California State University, Fullerton | Domain: VLSI Design & Testability

Abstract: At sub-micron semiconductor nodes, transition delay faults represent a major threat to circuit speed and reliability. This paper implements gate-level transition delay fault modeling and power dissipation analysis for an 8-bit Ripple Carry Adder. Utilizing H-Spice simulators at 32nm and 22nm CMOS nodes, the research evaluates delay vs. power trade-offs, sizing delay margins, and ATPG (Automated Test Pattern Generation) fault coverage to isolate transition timing faults.

H-Spice 32nm/22nm CMOS Transition Delay Faults ATPG Testing Low-Power VLSI

3. Remote IC Activation & Hardware Cryptography

Author: Swetang Gajjar | Institution: California State University, Fullerton | Domain: Hardware Security

Abstract: Globalized semiconductor manufacturing exposes supply chains to piracy and cloning risks. This research explores Remote Integrated Circuit Activation models utilizing physical unclonable functions (PUFs) and state-transition locking (Finite State Machine Cryptography). The paper details how remote activation servers can securely authenticate and unlock chips post-fabrication by dynamic state manipulation, preventing hardware counterfeit and intellectual property theft.

Hardware Security Physical Unclonable Functions (PUF) FSM Cryptography IC Activation Supply Chain Protection

4. Packet Queuing Behavior in Networks Using OMNeT++

Author: Swetang Gajjar | Institution: California State University, Fullerton | Domain: Computer Networks

Abstract: Simulated node delay, queue modeling, and Quality of Service (QoS) in high-throughput data networking topologies. Utilized the OMNeT++ simulation platform to build discrete-event network models, analyzing queue sizes, packet drop rates, and routing efficiency across congested layer-3 routing links.
